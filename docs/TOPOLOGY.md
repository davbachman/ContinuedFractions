# Covers, tangles, and markings

These features extend the original scope. Their coordinates describe two different tori. No conversion from a hyperbolic boundary endpoint to a homology slope is assumed.

## Source conventions checked

- [Springborn, _The hyperbolic geometry of Markov’s theorem_, §§12–14, especially §12 pp. 356–357](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/44352): the modular torus is the index-six commutator cover. The discussions of ideal triangulations and simple closed geodesics do not make an arbitrary vertical cusp-to-boundary ray a simple torus slope.
- [Kauffman–Lambropoulou, _On the Classification of Rational Tangles_, §2, Fig. 2 and pp. 5–7; coloring construction in §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0311499): zero and infinity, crossing types, counterclockwise rotation, reflection, and fraction conventions. Active rotation and reciprocal are distinct operations.
- [Kauffman–Lambropoulou, _On the Classification of Rational Knots_, §2 Fig. 2, Theorem 2, and Fig. 15 pp. 20–21](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0212011): numerator closures and their nonunique fractions. The paper explicitly identifies N(−3) and N(3/2) as the left-handed trefoil, fixing the mirror convention used here.

The atlas maps and coordinate calculations below are this application's constructions, not copied figures or claims of Euclidean isometry.

## Six-sheet hyperbolic cover

Matrices act on the left. The existing reducer stores R with w=Rz in F. The cover subgroup is K=ker χ with χ(T)=1 and χ(S)=3 in Z/6. Thus the sheet is **j=−χ(R) mod 6**, and TʲR belongs to K. Choosing a lift offset k uses j=k−χ(R); this changes the lift, not α.

The six domains are Tʲ(F), 0≤j<6. The outer vertical pairing is T⁶. For j=0,1,2, the circular pairing is Aⱼ=Tʲ⁺³ S T⁻ʲ; the reverse pairing is Aⱼ⁻¹, **not** Tʲ S T⁻⁽ʲ⁺³⁾ followed by an unrecorded deck shift. Both have χ=0. Point transport uses A and tangent transport uses v/(cz+d)². The seam transition between two reductions is `(Tʲ⁺ R⁺)(Tʲ⁻ R⁻)⁻¹`.

T permutes the six sheets in one cycle: one cusp of width six. S has three length-two cycles; ST has two length-three cycles; neither has fixed sheets, so there are no elliptic stabilizers. The six finite corners identify in classes {0,2,4} and {1,3,5}. Compactifying gives V−E+F=2−3+1=0; removing the single cusp gives χ=−1 and genus one. This differs from Γ(2), which contains T².

For the flat model let vⱼ=(cos(jπ/3),sin(jπ/3)), u=x+½ and ρ=√(1−x²)/y for w=x+iy in F. Map sheet j to `ρ((1−u)vⱼ+u vⱼ₊₁)`. Vertical seams agree along radial sides. On the circular boundary S sends u to 1−u and pairs opposite hexagonal edges by a constant translation. Their lattice is generated by b₁=(3/2,√3/2), b₂=(0,√3). Lattice coordinates are U=2x/3, V=y/√3−x/3. The center is the removed cusp. The finite corners remain two distinct points after identification.

Embedding periodic (U,V) on a standard torus supplies a schematic 3D display. It is a piecewise smooth topological atlas, not a hyperbolic isometry. Intrinsic tangent transformations are tested separately from the display's chart derivatives. Trail paths split at every reduction/chart change; identified endpoints agree in the 3D atlas. Filled shaded surfaces use 78% group opacity and a projected triangle-depth test to distinguish front and rear curve segments. T-paired edges are blue; S-paired edges are purple, matching the H² copies. Each three-spoke geodesic in a Farey triangle changes side type at its order-three vertex, so the color is split there. The small central cutaway in the mesh truncates the displayed cusp, not the surface itself.

## Marked rational tangles: frozen conventions

Endpoint order is NW, NE, SW, SE. Screen coordinates have x rightwards and y downwards.

- **0** joins NW–NE and SW–SE, with no crossings.
- **∞** joins NW–SW and NE–SE, with no crossings; it is an exact projective fraction 1/0.
- **H(+1)** appends a crossing at the right endpoint pair. The strand entering that crossing at its NW corner passes **over** the strand entering at SW, and exits at SE. This is the positive horizontal twist. H(−1) reverses over/under. Applying H(n) means n signed half-twists, in chronological order from left to right.
- **R** is an active counterclockwise quarter-turn in the plane: old NE→new NW, SE→NE, NW→SW, SW→SE. Over/under is preserved. R⁻¹ rotates clockwise. Both induce x↦−1/x on rational isotopy classes; two quarter-turns preserve the class but need not preserve the literal drawing.
- **Mirror** switches every crossing, maps x↦−x, and negates all signed twist counts. Combining mirror and R gives reciprocal; R alone does not.
- **Numerator closure** joins NW–NE outside the top and SW–SE outside the bottom, with no new crossings. Open endpoints remain marked. Infinity closes to an unknot; zero closes to the two-component unlink. For reduced finite p/q, odd p means a knot, even p a two-component link.

A diagram is generated by the recorded operations on two initial arcs, not by guessing a picture from p/q. Its signed expression is stored alongside the exact rational. Canonical-number edits intentionally regenerate an expression for the new value; tangle operations preserve the user's expression, including cancelling twists and actual rotations. Shared undo restores both.

### Independent coloring check

Give the zero tangle endpoint colors (a,b,c,d)=(0,0,1,1). At every crossing the under-out color equals twice the over color minus the under-in color. The endpoint identity is a+d=b+c. The diagram fraction is **(b−a)/(b−d)**.

For H(n), b′=b+n(b−d), d′=d+n(b−d), with a,c unchanged. Therefore the diagram fraction changes by +n. R permutes colors to (b,d,a,c), giving −1/x. These identities independently verify arithmetic, connectivity, active rotation, and the positive-crossing choice before rendering.

Closure fixtures: N(0) is the unlink; N(∞) and N(±1/q) are unknots; N(±2) are Hopf mirrors; N(3) is the right-handed trefoil, N(−3) and N(3/2) are left-handed; N(5/2) and N(−5/2) are the amphichiral figure-eight. Other closure names are not inferred. A closed knot/link never acts as a unique inverse fraction control.

## Separate branched-cover torus and pillowcase

This view is currently removed from the app pending a clearer visual design. The verified mathematical conventions below are retained as a reference for future work.

Use a **different** marked torus R²/Z² with basis eₓ,eᵧ. The involution is ι(U,V)=(−U,−V). Its four fixed points are (0,0),(½,0),(0,½),(½,½). Label their images NW, NE, SW, SE respectively. This marking is independent of the modular-cover hexagon and its sheet indices.

For a tangle fraction p/q, the primitive meridian direction is **(q,p)**. Choose two parallel curves `qV−pU=±1/4 mod 1`; ι exchanges them. Their common image in the pillowcase is the separating-disk boundary, avoiding all four branch points. The double branched cover of the tangle is a solid torus; either lifted curve bounds a meridian disk there.

The lifted positive endpoint half-twist is the Dehn shear D(U,V)=(U,V+U), sending (q,p) to (q,p+q) and x to x+1. It fixes NW and SW while exchanging NE and SE, exactly the right endpoint pair downstairs. The quarter-turn lift A(U,V)=(−V,U)+(0,½) sends (q,p) to (−p,q), and permutes the marked branch points exactly as active R. Its slope is −1/x. Thus these operations supply a verified bidirectional conversion for **this marking only**. They do not convert the hyperbolic endpoint α into a homology slope on the six-sheet cover.

For an irrational shared number, a chosen convergent supplies the finite tangle and marked slope, visibly labeled as an approximation. Editing either makes the resulting rational the shared number.

## Marked two-triangle flip editor

This is a marked torus R²/Z² with the fixed ordered basis (1,0), (0,1), labeled meridian and longitude. It is a combinatorial Farey model, separate from the modular commutator cover. The target number and the current triangulation are distinct state values. Selecting a rational target does not manually choose an edge, and a manual flip does not edit the target.

A slope p/q is stored as a normalized exact rational, representing the primitive vector (q,p) modulo simultaneous sign. A triangulation consists of three classes with pairwise absolute determinant one. For retained primitive vectors u,v, choose the sign of v so that u+v represents the removed edge. The replacement is u−v. The universal-cover quadrilateral has vertices 0,u,u+v,v, area one, and incident triangles (0,u,u+v), (0,v,u+v). Its diagonals are 0→u+v and u→v. This construction applies to every edge of the generalized one-vertex triangulation, including boundary-edge copies; it does not require an embedded quadrilateral in the displayed square.

Starting from {0,∞,1}, removing 1 introduces −1, removing 0 introduces 2, and removing ∞ introduces 1/2. The same retained pair identifies a Farey edge. Its two incident Farey triangles supply exactly the two possible diagonal classes. Applying the flip twice restores the slope triple and edge identities.

An edge is drawn from (0,0) to (q,p). Cut its parameter interval at the exact rational times k/q and k/|p|, then translate each open interval into the unit square using its midpoint's integer cell. Boundary endpoints stay on their corresponding sides; separate intervals are never joined across a chart change. Axis classes draw both identified boundary copies. Exact crossing times and normalized primitive classes, rather than floating-point slope ordering, decide the mathematics. The marked square keeps the fixed meridian–longitude basis throughout playback.

Follow-target mode reuses `fareyCrossings` directly. It starts in {a₀,∞,a₀+1}, and each recorded crossing retains its two endpoint slopes and flips the third. Crossing time and coefficient index are copied from the existing L/R coding map, including its initial and terminal offsets. A finite rational reaches a terminal _triangle incident to its cusp_; the ray never arrives at that cusp in finite time. Integer rays lie on vertical Farey edges, so their side ambiguity is disclosed and no arbitrary automatic flip occurs. Infinite and truncated itineraries are explicitly distinguished from rational termination.

The version 4 state stores the starting triple and a bounded sequence of records `{edge, source, time?, term?}`, plus cursor, mode, and horizon. Every slope triple and universal-cover quadrilateral is reconstructed exactly from those records. This preserves the information needed for a later layering extension without implementing tetrahedra here. Number and flip commands use the same scene history; target edits preserve manual triangulations and rebuild following itineraries atomically. H² play/reverse/scrubbing follows the stored crossing times, while manual path navigation remains independent of hyperbolic time.
